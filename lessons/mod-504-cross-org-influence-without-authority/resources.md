## Influence without authority — the canonical reading list

Chapter 1's working definition names *"influence without authority"* as the module's load-bearing phrase. The phrase has a specific body of literature behind it.

- Allan R. Cohen and David L. Bradford, *Influence Without Authority* (Wiley, 3rd edition 2017). The textbook that gave the phrase its canonical meaning. The *law of reciprocity* and the *currencies-of-exchange* framework underlie chapter 3 § *the peer's authority is what you borrow, not what you replace*.
- Allan R. Cohen and David L. Bradford, *Influencing Up* (Wiley, 2012) — the shorter companion volume focused on upward influence; chapter 2's SVP / CTO altitude is the direct audience.
- Jay A. Conger, *"The Necessary Art of Persuasion"*, *Harvard Business Review* (May–June 1998). The HBR classic that enumerates the four stages of effective organisational persuasion. Reads adjacent to chapter 4's four-stage coalition sequence; the HBR frame is more rhetoric-oriented, chapter 4's is engineering-oriented. [HBR archive](https://hbr.org/1998/05/the-necessary-art-of-persuasion).
- Robert B. Cialdini, *Influence: The Psychology of Persuasion* (Harper Business, revised edition 2021). The six principles (reciprocity, commitment / consistency, social proof, authority, liking, scarcity) are the vocabulary that underlies chapter 4 § stage B's shift-signal discipline, even though chapter 4 does not invoke them by name.
- Herminia Ibarra, *Act Like a Leader, Think Like a Leader* (Harvard Business Review Press, 2015). The *outsight* framing and the *stepping-up* principle underlie chapter 1 § *not a stepping stone to VP* — the IC-principal seat has its own identity work.

## Decision-making and reversibility — chapter 6's protocol provenance

The three-way question (escalate / grind / defer) and the deferred-decision artifact draw from a specific body of decision-theory literature.

- Jeff Bezos, [2015 shareholder letter](https://www.aboutamazon.com/news/company-news/2015-letter-to-shareholders) — the *one-way-door vs two-way-door* decisions framework. A deferred decision is a two-way door kept open; an escalation to an executive altitude typically converts a one-way door that would otherwise close by default.
- Daniel Kahneman, *Thinking, Fast and Slow* (Farrar, Straus and Giroux, 2011). Chapters on prospect theory, loss aversion, and the planning fallacy underlie chapter 6 § *dead-scope discipline* — the discipline of writing off a bet is the discipline of fighting sunk-cost bias.
- Annie Duke, *Thinking in Bets* (Portfolio, 2018) and *Quit* (Portfolio, 2022). *Quit* is especially aligned with chapter 6 § *dead-scope discipline* — the discipline of *knowing when to stop* as a learnable skill.
- Patrick Lencioni, *Death by Meeting* (Jossey-Bass, 2004). The four-meeting discipline (daily check-in, weekly tactical, monthly strategic, quarterly off-site) is the cadence-pattern antecedent for chapter 3's four counterparts' cadences.

## Standards and regulatory frames — for the FAQ sections

Chapter 5's FAQ section 4 (*Head of AI Governance objections*) presumes the regulatory landscape the Head of AI Governance operates in. Full citation treatment is in [`../mod-503-north-star-applied-ml-architecture/resources.md`](../mod-503-north-star-applied-ml-architecture/resources.md); the FAQ-authoring references are:

- European Union, [*AI Act (Regulation 2024/1689)*](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=OJ:L_202401689). Risk-tier classification, high-risk-system obligations, GPAI (general-purpose AI) downstream-deployer obligations. The regulatory objections a Head of AI Governance will raise at the strategy ratification table are mapped against these.
- NIST, [*AI Risk Management Framework 1.0*](https://www.nist.gov/itl/ai-risk-management-framework) and the [*Generative AI Profile overlay (NIST AI 600-1)*](https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/ai/NIST.AI.600-1.pdf). The RMF's four functions (Govern / Map / Measure / Manage) are the shape of the Head of AI Governance's objection categories in the FAQ.
- ISO/IEC 42001:2023 — *Information technology — Artificial intelligence — Management system*. [ISO standard page](https://www.iso.org/standard/81230.html). The AI-management-system standard the governance peer may be aligning against.
