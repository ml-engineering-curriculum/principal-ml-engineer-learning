# What "Influence Without Authority" Means at Principal Scope

> Chapter 1 of mod-504. Prerequisite: mod-501 chapters 1, 4, and 5 (verb-and-object vocabulary, three-role-families boundary, the four principal-scope themes) and mod-502 chapters 1 and 7 (definition of the strategy artifact and the paired one-pager). Downstream: every subsequent chapter refines one clause of the definition this chapter fixes.

## Why start with the definition

*Influence* is the load-bearing verb of the third principal-scope theme (mod-501 chapter 5). Unlike *own* or *set*, it names an action that has to succeed **without** a mechanism the IC-principal is guaranteed to have — no direct reports, no line authority, no budget, no formal veto over anyone else's roadmap. Everything the principal accomplishes across the ML function is transmitted through some other person who chose to act. The chapter's job is to name what makes that transmission reliable enough to bet a multi-year direction on.

Four postings in the 2026-Q2 sample carry the load for this module. Read them together — each cuts a different failure mode.

- **Capital One (Sr. Distinguished AI Engineer, Remote-Eligible):** *"Demonstrated ability to lead and mentor an engineering organization and influence cross-functional stakeholders up to the SVP level."* The audience ceiling — *SVP* — is the load-bearing word here. Chapter 2 walks the ceiling.
- **Bumble (Principal ML Engineer, Matching and Recommendations):** *"Operate as a senior individual contributor, influencing technical strategy and decision-making without direct authority."* The mechanism ceiling — *without direct authority* — is stated in the same sentence as the identity (*senior individual contributor*). Chapter 3 walks the contract that replaces the authority.
- **Twilio (Principal ML Engineer):** *"Lead cross-functional engineering efforts, breaking down complex initiatives into executable roadmaps."* The output ceiling — *executable roadmaps* — is the acceptance test. Influence that never becomes an executable roadmap is not influence at principal altitude. Chapter 4 walks the coalition sequence that produces it.
- **Wiser Solutions (Principal ML Engineer):** *"Define and evolve the AI and data science technical strategy in partnership with product and business leadership."* The partnership frame — *in partnership with* — cuts the "solo genius" failure mode. Chapter 5 walks the narrative craft the partnership hinges on.

The four sentences point at the same object from four angles: an IC-principal who reliably gets the ML function's technical direction ratified up to SVP / CTO / CEO altitude and executed across product organizations, without ever having reporting authority over the humans who do the executing.

## Working definition

Cross-org influence without authority, at IC-principal altitude, is:

> The reliable, repeatable practice of getting the ML org's technical direction ratified and executed across product organizations up to SVP / CTO / CEO altitude — from the IC-principal seat, without line authority over the executing teams — through an audience-matched written artifact, a named partnership contract with the peer executive branch, a coalition sequenced on an engineering cadence, and an escalation-and-deferral protocol that separates blockers from deferrals from dead scope.

Every clause is load-bearing.

- **Reliable, repeatable practice** — not a heroic one-off. An IC-principal who can move one bet by force of personality but cannot describe how the *next* one will move has a story, not a practice. The distinction is what makes the skill hireable at $250K–$500K comp bands (mod-501 chapter 3 title map).
- **Ratified and executed** — two words, not one. Ratification is the SVP / CTO nodding at the room; execution is the executable roadmap that lands. Ratification without execution is the "consensus theater" failure mode; execution without ratification is the "cowboy" failure mode. Chapter 4 owns the seam between them.
- **Up to SVP / CTO / CEO altitude** — the audience ceiling is the differentiator. Staff-scope influence tops out at VP / Director of Product / peer platform VP; principal-scope influence extends to SVP / CTO / CEO / board sub-committee. Chapter 2 fixes the boundary.
- **From the IC-principal seat, without line authority** — the mechanism constraint. This is what separates this module from any executive-branch track. If the answer to *"how did you get that decision made?"* is *"I directed my reports to"*, the skill being demonstrated is executive-branch, not IC-principal. Chapter 3 owns the substitute mechanism (the executive-branch partnership contract).
- **Audience-matched written artifact** — every ratification runs through a document. The SVP does not sit through your 30-slide deck; they read a page. Chapter 5 owns the artifact craft.
- **Named partnership contract with the peer executive branch** — the CAIO, Head of AI Governance, CISO, and CFO are peers whose formal authority you borrow. The contract with each is *named and versioned*, not a "good relationship". Chapter 3 owns the four contracts.
- **Coalition sequenced on an engineering cadence** — the deliberate, staged sequence of pre-read → one-on-one → decision-room → follow-up. Chapter 4 walks the sequence as engineering practice, not a soft skill.
- **Escalation-and-deferral protocol that separates blockers from deferrals from dead scope** — the discipline that keeps the principal from either grinding indefinitely on a dead bet or escalating a bet the org will simply refuse. Chapter 6 owns it.

Every subsequent chapter in this module sharpens one of those clauses.

## The four postings, unpacked

The composite target is worth reading one clause at a time. Each posting cuts a distinct failure mode; the fact that all four coexist in the 2026-Q2 sample is what tells you the target is a single practice, not four separate skills.

**Capital One — the audience ceiling.** *SVP* is not shorthand for *senior leaders*. It names a specific altitude: two rungs above a Director, one rung above a VP, adjacent-to-CTO in most org charts, and typically the level at which an ML bet crosses from a functional decision into a portfolio-wide investment allocation. Reading *SVP* as *VP-and-above* collapses the audience distinction chapter 2 fixes. In the same posting family Capital One also carries a *Distinguished* posting that reads *"up to the VP level"* — the two are deliberately titled at different levels, and the audience ceiling difference is one of the tells.

**Bumble — the mechanism ceiling.** *Without direct authority* is doing double work. First, it disambiguates the ladder (IC, not people-management — mod-501 chapter 4). Second, it names the constraint the whole module is organised around: the *how* cannot be *because I said so as your manager*. Every mechanism in this module — the artifact, the partnership contract, the coalition sequence, the escalation protocol — is a substitute for line authority. The Bumble framing is the cleanest single-sentence statement of the constraint.

**Twilio — the output ceiling.** *Executable roadmaps* names the acceptance test. A coalition that never becomes a roadmap teams can execute is a hallway conversation. The Twilio sentence sets a high bar: not a strategy document, not a decision memo, but an executable roadmap. That word — *executable* — is chapter 4's forcing function. Coalition-building that stops at ratification and does not carry through to commitment-tracked execution is one of the module's named failure modes.

**Wiser — the partnership frame.** *In partnership with product and business leadership* cuts the myth that the IC-principal produces the strategy solo and then "socialises" it. The IC-principal *co-authors* the strategy with product and business leadership — those partners bring the P&L accountability, market context, and executive-branch mandate that the IC-principal does not have. Chapter 3 walks the partnership contract in the four peer directions (CAIO, governance, CISO, CFO); Wiser's sentence is the load-bearing statement that the partnership frame is not optional.

Read the four sentences together and the audience, the mechanism, the output, and the working frame are all fixed. The rest of the module is craft.

## The five defensibility tests

A principal-scope influence practice is defensible when it passes all five of these tests. Use them as a checklist against your own portfolio — the ratified-and-executed bets that carry your history — and against the artifacts this module produces.

1. **Technical-voice legitimacy.** When you sit in the SVP / CTO room as the ML function's technical voice, the room defers to your framing of the technical question — not because of your title, but because the framing has been credible over multiple prior cycles. This is what the mod-505 technical-bar work funds: a principal who has been *right on the technical calls* over years is the principal whose framing gets deferred to. Without it, chapter 5's narrative craft is form without weight.
2. **Audience-matched artifact.** For every ratification you seek, the artifact matches the audience. SVP / CTO gets a one-pager. VP / Director gets a deep-dive. Staff engineers get the mod-410 exec-audience RFC. If the same document is used for all three, chapter 2's audience boundary has not landed.
3. **Executive-branch partnership contract.** The four peer executives (CAIO / Head of AI, Head of AI Governance, CISO, CFO) each have a named partnership contract with you — cadence, decision rights, escalation path, artifact-flow. When one of them is asked *"what does the IC-principal bring you and what do you bring them?"* they can answer in one paragraph. If not, chapter 3 has not been done.
4. **Coalition sequenced on an engineering cadence.** For every material bet you have moved, you can name the pre-read distribution list, the one-on-one prep notes for each executive, the decision-room agenda, and the follow-up commitment tracker. This is the same discipline as engineering a system; the tracker is the code. If the coalition looks retrospectively narratable but is not prospectively legible, chapter 4 has not been done.
5. **Escalation-and-deferral discipline.** When a bet does not move, you can name in one sentence which of the three states it is in — *escalated* (a specific executive owns the decision by a named date), *grinding* (a specific technical unblock is pending, with a named signal that would trigger re-open), *deferred* (a specific revisit condition names the next cycle) — and the artifact that records the state. Without this discipline, dead bets soak your energy and deferred bets get lost.

If a portfolio fails any one of the five, the fix is usually earlier in the practice than the failure appears. Legitimacy failures come from the technical bar (mod-505); audience-mismatch failures come from confusing the artifact tiers (chapter 2); partnership-contract failures come from treating the executive branch as an escalation target rather than a peer (chapter 3); coalition-legibility failures come from treating coalition-building as a soft skill (chapter 4); deferral-discipline failures come from the missing revisit criteria (chapter 6).

## What "influence without authority" is *not*

Each of the following is a real, useful skill. None of them is what this module is about. Naming them explicitly is how you keep the definition sharp.

### Not mentorship

Mod-505 owns mentorship and technical-bar calibration for the ML org. Mentorship is directional (senior to less-senior), private (in a 1:1), and about the growth of the mentee. Cross-org influence is peer-directional or upward (principal to VP / SVP / CTO / peer executive), public (in a decision-room), and about the ratification of a bet.

Signs a portfolio has confused the two: the "influence" evidence is a list of engineers coached to promotion, with no cross-org ratification stories. Mentorship is real work; it is chapter-5 mod-501 theme 4, owned by mod-505. It is not what this module practises.

### Not soft-skills-only stakeholder management

Stakeholder management as taught in general leadership curricula — active listening, empathy, meeting facilitation, conflict de-escalation — is a *component* of what this module walks, but it is not the whole thing. This module treats the mechanics: the artifact, the contract, the sequence, the protocol. If a curriculum's answer to *"how do I influence a CTO?"* is *"be an active listener"*, the mechanics are missing.

Signs a portfolio has confused the two: the practice descriptions are heavy on the tone (*"build trust"*, *"listen deeply"*, *"be authentic"*) and light on the artifact and the sequence. Tone matters and is necessary; it is not sufficient. Chapter 4 walks the sequence; the sequence is what makes the practice repeatable.

### Not a stepping stone to VP

The IC-principal seat is the destination, not a waystation. Mod-501 chapter 4 (three role families) is explicit: the exec-branch trajectory is a distinct role family (`chief-ai-officer-learning`, `head-of-ai-governance-learning`), with a distinct interview loop and a distinct multi-year investment. An IC-principal who is running the practice in this module *as a VP audition* has misread the module.

The practical failure mode: the "influence" moves stop being about ratifying the bet and start being about being *seen ratifying the bet*. The audience notices, and the technical-voice legitimacy from test 1 erodes. Chapter 4 § *coalition-of-one failure mode* is where this shows up most visibly.

If your target trajectory is exec-branch, most of this module still applies (a technical executive still runs coalitions and holds partnership contracts) but layer `chief-ai-officer-learning` on top; do not substitute this module for it.

### Not "just be very persuasive"

The reduction of influence to persuasion — the framing that a good enough one-pager and a good enough talk track will carry the room — is the modal failure this module exists to correct. Persuasion is a small part of chapter 5 (the narrative craft) and a much smaller part of chapters 3, 4, and 6. The vast majority of the ratification work happens *before* the room, in the one-on-ones, and *after* the room, in the commitment tracking. A principal who walks into a decision-room hoping to persuade the executives on the spot has not done the work.

Signs a portfolio has confused the two: the influence stories are all set in the room ("*I walked into the SVP review, opened with a strong hook, and the room agreed*"). The rooms where decisions get made almost always have the decision already sequenced through the one-on-ones; the room ratifies what has already been converged. Chapter 4 § *the room the decision is actually made in* is the reference.

## The paired-artifact frame, extended

Mod-502 chapter 7 fixed the paired-artifact frame for the strategy document: a one-pager (executive audience) plus a deep-dive companion (staff-engineering audience). Mod-503 chapter 1 named the paired north-star architecture (living diagrams plus canonical APIs) that pairs with the strategy. This module adds a third layer: the paired practice that moves both.

| Artifact tier                                       | Owner        | Audience                                             | Instrument                                  | Reference                                   |
| --------------------------------------------------- | ------------ | ---------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------- |
| Multi-year ML technical North Star (strategy)       | Principal    | CTO / SVP + staff engineers (paired one-pager / deep-dive) | Written                                     | mod-502                                     |
| North-star applied-ML architecture                  | Principal    | Whole ML org + platform peers                        | Living diagrams + canonical APIs            | mod-503                                     |
| **The practice that ratifies and executes both**    | **Principal (this module)** | **SVP / CTO peer set + peer executive branch + coalition of ML / platform / product / governance / finance leaders** | **Audience-matched artifact + partnership contracts + coalition sequence + escalation protocol** | **mod-504 (this module)** |

The three artifact tiers cite each other. The strategy names the bet; the architecture names the seams the bet plugs into; the influence practice ratifies and executes both. An IC-principal who has authored the first two but cannot demonstrate the third has produced documents that do not move.

The three-tier frame is the mental model the rest of the module works inside. Chapter 5's one-pager is the SVP / CTO-audience *lift* of the strategy one-pager; chapter 3's partnership contracts are the *practice* of holding the seams chapter 8 of mod-503 named; chapter 4's coalition sequence is the *engineering discipline* around the ratification. Every chapter attaches to a mod-502 or mod-503 anchor and lifts it into practice.

## A worked micro-example

For grounding, here is what the *practice-summary paragraph* of a principal's cross-org influence portfolio reads like at defensible altitude. The full portfolio is chapter 2's audience calibration times chapter 3's four partnership contracts times chapter 4's coalition sequence times chapter 5's SVP / CTO-audience artifact times chapter 6's escalation protocol.

> **Practice (FY26 baseline, v3.2).** Over FY25–FY26 the IC-principal ratified three material ML bets — the Gen-AI-as-integration bet, the RecSys re-platform bet, and the deferral of foundation-model pretraining — across the ML function, two adjacent platform organizations, and the responsible-AI review body. Ratification was through the paired SVP / CTO-audience one-pager (v3.2, refreshed semi-annually) and the deep-dive companion, walked through in the CTO's quarterly technical council; a standing partnership contract with the Head of AI Governance, the CISO, and the CFO frames the four executive-branch dependencies; a coalition tracker with 17 named stakeholders across four VP directions carries the current-cycle asks; and the escalation-and-deferral log records the three currently-escalated items, the two grinding items, and the four deferred items with named revisit criteria. Two bets slated in FY24 have been retired to the *dead scope* list per chapter 6 discipline; the retirement was ratified by the same executive coalition that had originally ratified them.

Notice what is *not* in the paragraph: adjectives about relationships, stories about persuasion in the room, references to individual charisma. What *is* in it: the artifact version, the four counterparts, the coalition tracker, the escalation log, the retirement discipline. That is the standard the practice has to hit.

A CTO reading this paragraph can figure out whether the principal is a peer they would ratify a bet through. A peer executive (CAIO, Head of AI Governance) can figure out what the principal expects from them and what they get in return. A staff engineer can figure out whether the direction their team is executing against was genuinely ratified. That is the standard the influence practice has to hit.

## Summary

- Cross-org influence without authority at principal altitude is a reliable, repeatable practice of getting the ML org's technical direction ratified and executed across product organizations up to SVP / CTO / CEO altitude, from the IC-principal seat, through an audience-matched written artifact, a named partnership contract with the peer executive branch, a coalition sequenced on an engineering cadence, and an escalation-and-deferral protocol.
- The 2026-Q2 target framing is the composite of four postings: Capital One (audience ceiling — *SVP*), Bumble (mechanism ceiling — *without direct authority*), Twilio (output ceiling — *executable roadmaps*), Wiser (working frame — *in partnership with*). Every subsequent chapter refines one clause.
- The practice is *not* mentorship (owned by mod-505), *not* soft-skills-only stakeholder management, *not* a stepping stone to VP (owned by exec-branch tracks), and *not* "just be very persuasive". The reductions each obscure a specific mechanism the module treats.
- Five defensibility tests every practice passes: technical-voice legitimacy, audience-matched artifact, executive-branch partnership contract, coalition sequenced on an engineering cadence, escalation-and-deferral discipline.
- The three-tier paired-artifact frame extends mod-502 (strategy) and mod-503 (architecture) with this module's tier: the practice that ratifies and executes both. Every chapter attaches to a mod-502 or mod-503 anchor. Chapter 2 draws the SVP / CTO vs. VP audience boundary next.
