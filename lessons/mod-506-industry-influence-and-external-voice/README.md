# mod-506-industry-influence-and-external-voice: Industry Influence & External Voice — Publications, Keynotes, Open Source, and the References Others Cite

**Estimated effort:** 10 hours

This module owns the fifth of the principal-scope themes named in the [`CURRICULUM.md`](../../CURRICULUM.md) — the *external-voice / industry-influence facet* the role definition names as principal-defining. The target framings are Capital One's *"ML industry impact through conference presentations, papers, blog posts, or open source contributions"* (Sr. Distinguished AI Engineer, preferred qualification) and Ibotta's *"act as a leading contributor, voice, and thought leader in a growing community of machine learning practitioners"* (Principal ML Engineer). Every chapter treats one clause of that composite target.

The module is held at 10 hours — the smallest in the plan — because the underlying requirement (`req-industry-influence`) is a *preferred* qualification (freq 0.15 in the 26-posting sample, below the 0.30 material threshold). Grounded in role definition rather than in-window frequency. A principal who has never spoken externally can still hold the seat; a principal who has *only* spoken externally cannot. Chapter 2 argues why.

Read the chapters in order. Chapter 1 fixes external influence as engineering work with a venue-audience-artifact matching discipline. Chapter 2 comes second (even though it maps to the fifth learning objective) because the internal-community substrate must precede and continue in parallel with any external voice. Chapters 3–5 walk each of the three externally-visible venue classes at principal altitude (paper / blog, keynote, open source). Chapter 6 closes the loop with the return-on-external-influence tracking and the retirement criteria.

## Learning objectives

- Frame external industry influence as an engineering discipline: pick the venue (ICML / NeurIPS / KDD industry track, O'Reilly AI, QCon, an applied-ML engineering blog, an open-source contribution) that fits the artifact and the audience — per Capital One 'ML industry impact through conference presentations, papers, blog posts, or open source contributions' as an explicit preferred qualification.
- Author the industry-track paper or blog that extracts a principal-scope lesson from a real production ML system — architecture at portfolio-and-industry altitude, cross-team eval program discipline, org-wide bet framing, foundation-model program economics. Ground in the productive-dissemination pattern (Papers With Code newsletter, the Meta / Airbnb / Uber / Pinterest applied-ML engineering blog series) — not in academic publishing craft, which belongs to the peer research-principal path.
- Deliver a conference keynote or industry-track talk: pick the through-line, the concrete artifact the audience can take away, and the FAQ the room will bring; treat the talk as an executive-audience artifact whose audience is the industry rather than the CTO. Reuse the mod-504 executive-narrative craft.
- Contribute to open source at principal altitude — the release-level decisions (what to open-source vs. keep proprietary, what the maintenance burden looks like, how to align with the internal north-star architecture) rather than the day-to-day commits. Ground in the maintainership contract that peer specialist tracks build against.
- Act as internal-community thought leader — per Ibotta 'act as a leading contributor, voice, and thought leader in a growing community of machine learning practitioners' — via internal talks, tech-radar authorship, a canonical org-wide reference doc series. Distinguish this from external industry influence: the internal signal compounds first, and it must precede external influence for the principal to stay grounded.
- Track the return on external-influence investment: the recruiting signal (staff+ hires who cite the talk / paper as a reason for interviewing), the industry-standard adoption (peer companies citing the reference architecture), and the internal leverage (junior staff building on the principal's canonical write-up). Retire an influence bet whose signal has decayed.

## Chapters

1. [`01-external-influence-as-engineering-discipline.md`](01-external-influence-as-engineering-discipline.md) — external influence is engineering work with an artifact, audience, cadence, and review body. The four venue classes Capital One enumerates and what each does. The venue-audience-artifact matching discipline (four steps). Six venue-selection failure modes. The bridge to chapter 2's internal-first substrate.
2. [`02-internal-community-thought-leadership-precedes-external.md`](02-internal-community-thought-leadership-precedes-external.md) — Ibotta's *thought leader in a growing community* framing scopes internal-first. Three internal-authored artifact classes at principal altitude (internal talk, tech-radar entry, canonical org-wide reference doc). The 70/30 internal-first ratio and its rebalancing signals. Co-authorship as leverage and succession. Internal vs. external distinctions (access, IP surface, reviewer, feedback horizon). Six failure modes.
3. [`03-industry-track-paper-or-blog-craft.md`](03-industry-track-paper-or-blog-craft.md) — the two venues share more craft than they differ. Four principal-scope lesson shapes. Three signals for paper vs. blog (numeric load-bearing, lesson-still-evolving, org legal / IP posture). The lesson-extraction discipline (four steps). Paper shape (8–12 pages) and blog shape (2,000–5,000 words). Shared writing discipline (traces, honest failure, no gratuitous proper nouns, co-authorship). The paper-plus-blog fan-out. Pre-publication legal review. Six failure modes.
4. [`04-conference-keynote-and-industry-track-talk.md`](04-conference-keynote-and-industry-track-talk.md) — the keynote is an executive-audience artifact with the industry substituted for the CTO. The venue map (plenary / industry-track / invited / workshop / panel). The three-part artifact: through-line (one-sentence memorable), take-away (concrete Monday-morning artifact), FAQ (the room's anticipated objections). Explicit reuse map from mod-504 chapter 5. The hallway track (coalition-sequencing lifted to conference venue). Talk-authoring discipline (six steps). Panel variant. Eight failure modes.
5. [`05-open-source-at-principal-altitude.md`](05-open-source-at-principal-altitude.md) — release-level decisions the principal owns; commit-level craft the peer specialist tracks own. Four decisions: what to release vs. keep proprietary, the maintenance contract (seven commitments), the north-star alignment posture, the exit / hand-off shape. Six right-shapes and six wrong-shapes for open-sourcing. The two-implementations trap. The maintainership contract with peer specialist tracks. The release-proposal artifact (10 sections, 5–15 pages). Eight failure modes.
6. [`06-return-on-external-influence-and-retirement-criteria.md`](06-return-on-external-influence-and-retirement-criteria.md) — the three return signals: recruiting-funnel citation, industry-standard adoption, internal leverage. The quarterly tracking dashboard (one row per artifact, one column per signal per quarter). The calendar-shape check that keeps the internal-first ratio honest. Four retirement triggers. Interaction with the mod-505 personal-cadence artifact. Seven failure modes.

## Exercises

Four exercises. Each builds one of the four artifacts a principal-scope external-influence portfolio needs to have on hand.

- [`exercises/exercise-01-industry-influence-artifact-plan.md`](exercises/exercise-01-industry-influence-artifact-plan.md) — 2h. Author the fiscal-year artifact plan for the next 12 months. Applies chapter 1's venue-audience-artifact discipline, chapter 2's internal-first ratio, and chapter 6's tracking dashboard end-to-end. This is the *plan* the other three exercises produce artifacts against.
- [`exercises/exercise-02-industry-track-paper-or-blog-draft.md`](exercises/exercise-02-industry-track-paper-or-blog-draft.md) — 3h. Draft one paper or blog end-to-end per chapter 3. Pick the lesson, extract from the internal substrate, produce a full first draft, run the pre-publication checklist.
- [`exercises/exercise-03-conference-keynote-outline-and-faq.md`](exercises/exercise-03-conference-keynote-outline-and-faq.md) — 3h. Author the through-line, the take-away artifact, the FAQ, and the section-level outline per chapter 4. Rehearse to time; capture the pre-flight readout.
- [`exercises/exercise-04-principal-scope-open-source-contribution-plan.md`](exercises/exercise-04-principal-scope-open-source-contribution-plan.md) — 2h. Author the release-proposal artifact per chapter 5 for one candidate release from the org's system portfolio. Ratify (or self-simulate) the maintenance contract, the alignment posture, and the exit shape.
