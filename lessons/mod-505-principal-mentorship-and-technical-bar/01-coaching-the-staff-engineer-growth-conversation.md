# Coaching the Staff Engineer's Growth Conversation

> Chapter 1 of mod-505. Prerequisite: mod-501 chapters 3 (title map), 4 (three role families boundary), 5 (four principal-scope themes, § *Theme 4*), and 6 (hand-off contracts back to staff scope). Downstream: chapters 2–6 assume you can hold the L40→? conversation without collapsing into either the mentor-your-favorites failure mode or the shadow-EM failure mode this chapter names.

## Why start with the L40 growth conversation

Mod-501 chapter 5 § *Theme 4* named the object of the *coach* verb at principal altitude: **staff engineers (L40) and staff-plus hiring committees**. Not senior tech leads — that object belongs to the level below (`staff-ml-engineer-learning` mod-410 exercise-04 owns coaching the L30 senior IC through their first multi-team blueprint). Not directs — the IC-principal has none. The single most consequential 1:1 the principal runs, in a given quarter, is the growth conversation with a staff engineer who is trying to decide *what to become next*.

That conversation is load-bearing for three reasons.

- **The choice fans out.** A staff engineer picking the *principal-IC* path is signing up for another 3–5 years in scope like yours. A staff engineer picking the *engineering-manager* path is leaving your reporting line for the peer-EM branch. A staff engineer picking the *specialist* path is going deep on a technical axis (RecSys architect, ML performance engineer, ML security lead) that will still be an IC but will pattern-match to a distinct hireable archetype. Getting the fan-out wrong means either the wrong person on the principal-IC ladder (chapter 2's leverage-moves practice collapses), the wrong person in a first-line EM seat (peer-EM branch — the `ai-infra-team-lead-learning` track — pays for that), or a strong specialist grinding on scope that does not match what they are actually good at.
- **The person is picking the archetype, not you.** The principal's job on this conversation is *not* to decide for them; it is to make the choice legible. The three paths look nearly identical from L40 (they all "get harder", they all "involve more scope") and radically different from L48+. Chapter 1 walks the three archetypes as they actually operate at the next level so the mentee can pattern-match against what they want to be doing at 45.
- **You are one of the few people in the room qualified to hold this conversation.** The staff engineer's line manager (an EM) can name what the *EM* path looks like from the inside but cannot name what the *principal-IC* or *specialist* path looks like at the next level with the same fidelity. The staff engineer's peer staff engineers can name what *staff* looks like but not what comes after. You have taken the principal-IC path yourself; you have watched at least one peer take the EM path; you have watched at least one peer take the specialist path. That is the sample the mentee needs.

The rest of this chapter fixes: what the three paths *are* at L48+; the four evidence axes the principal reads against; the *endorsement criteria* — the specific signal you would need to see before endorsing any of the three; the shape of the conversation itself; and the failure modes.

## The three paths as they operate at L48+

Mod-501 chapter 4 (three role families boundary) drew the line at level 48+ between IC-principal engineering, executive branch (Head of AI / CAIO), and research-principal. The staff engineer is *not* yet choosing between those three; they are choosing the *route* they take to the next level, and the routes fan into different destinations.

### The principal-IC path

**Destination.** The role this whole track owns: L48+ IC-principal engineering. No direct reports. Owns the ML org's technical direction across product areas, the north-star architecture, cross-org influence up to SVP / CTO, and the technical-bar work this module is *about*. Mod-501 chapter 5 § *four themes* is the shape.

**What the day looks like at the next level.** Roughly: a third of the week on the North Star / architecture artifacts and the reviews that keep them alive (mod-502, mod-503, chapter 4 of this module). A third on cross-org influence — the 1:1s that feed the executive-branch partnership contracts, the coalition-building around named bets, the SVP / CTO artifacts (mod-504). A third on the technical-bar work — this module. Very little time coding a specific system end-to-end after month one; more time coding *skeletons* other teams fill in and *evals* that discipline other teams' work.

**What makes the transition hard.** The staff engineer at L40 has been rewarded for delivery. The IC-principal at L48+ is rewarded for *leverage* (chapter 2). The gap between "delivered the multi-team blueprint" and "authored the reference architecture the whole ML org converges on" is not a size gap; it is an *audience* gap and an *artifact-durability* gap. The mentee needs to see themselves growing into the paired-artifact craft (mod-502 chapter 7, mod-503 chapter 5) — not just producing bigger versions of the artifacts they already produce.

### The engineering-manager path

**Destination.** A first-line or second-line EM seat — the peer people-management branch. Owned by [`ai-infra-team-lead-learning`](https://github.com/ai-infra-curriculum/ai-infra-team-lead-learning) at the entry rung; the second-line and director rungs live in adjacent people-management tracks. Direct reports, performance reviews, headcount, hiring plan, promo decisions, comp calibration.

**What the day looks like at the next level.** 1:1s. Cross-functional planning meetings. Roadmap negotiation with peer EMs and product. Performance-review calibration. Comp calibration. Hiring loops from the *hiring-manager* seat (not the calibrator seat you sit in — chapter 3). Some architecture / design review, but the calendar shape is fundamentally different from the IC-principal day.

**What makes the transition hard.** The reward function inverts. The staff engineer at L40 is optimizing for *their own technical output*; the first-line EM is optimizing for *their team's output* and — critically — for the growth of the people on their team. The engineers you are managing are not there to unblock you; you are there to unblock them. Mentees who take this path expecting to keep coding at L40 volume tend to bounce back to IC within 12–18 months. Mentees who take it expecting to *stop* coding tend to lose credibility with their engineers within 6 months. The path selects for someone whose energy is *replenished* by the people work, not merely tolerated.

### The specialist path

**Destination.** A senior IC role deep on a specific technical axis, often carrying a Distinguished, Principal, or Senior Staff title *scoped to* that axis — Distinguished RecSys Architect, Principal ML Performance Engineer, Distinguished Applied Research Scientist, Principal ML Security Engineer, Distinguished Feature-Platform Architect. The role families for these live in the peer specialist tracks — `senior-ml-engineer-learning`, `training-pipeline-engineer-learning`, `fine-tuning-engineer-learning`, `agentic-systems-architect-learning`, `rag-engineer-learning`, `ai-infra-performance-learning`, and the security-adjacent tracks.

The specialist path *is* an IC path, but it is not this track. The market pays for it at Principal / Distinguished title bands — sometimes higher than a generalist IC-principal for the same years, because the depth is genuinely hard to hire. Mod-501 chapter 3 (title map) is worth re-reading with the specialist track in mind: a *Distinguished RecSys Architect* is not the same job as a *Principal ML Engineer* even if the pay bands overlap.

**What the day looks like at the next level.** Deep single-axis technical work. Multi-year commitment to a specific problem class. External-community leadership on that axis (specialist conferences, standards bodies, open-source libraries). Cross-cutting reviews *within the axis* rather than across the ML org. The paired-artifact discipline still applies but the artifacts are axis-scoped (a *canonical RecSys reference architecture* rather than the whole ML org's North Star).

**What makes the transition hard.** The specialist has to keep *depth* current while accepting that the *breadth* of the IC-principal path will erode. Mentees who take the specialist path but keep saying yes to breadth work end up with the workload of a Principal and the depth of neither. The path selects for someone whose *identity* is tied to the axis, not to the seniority label.

## The four evidence axes to read the mentee against

Before you can hold the conversation, you have to read the mentee. Four axes cover the space; a fifth (personal-life fit) is the mentee's, not yours.

- **Energy pattern.** What kind of work leaves them *charged* at the end of the day vs. what leaves them *drained*? A staff engineer who is charged by writing the artifact and drained by the 1:1 that ratifies it is being told something about the IC path. A staff engineer who is charged by the 1:1 in which a struggling report figures out their own promo case and drained by the artifact is being told something about the EM path. A staff engineer who is charged by going three layers deeper on the ranking-loss decomposition and drained by the coalition meeting is being told something about the specialist path. This is not preference; it is signal. Ask them.
- **Portfolio evidence.** What are the actual artifacts on their staff-scope portfolio (their mod-410 exercise-02 exec-audience RFC portfolio, their mod-407 portfolio TCO, their multi-team blueprint from mod-402)? The IC path wants to see paired-artifact instincts — writing that separates the executive summary from the depth, revising the same document over multiple cycles, engaging with review comments substantively. The EM path wants to see evidence of *systems that scaled through the people* — the 1:1 notes with L20 / L30 engineers they coached, the retros they ran when a project shipped late, the ability to describe another engineer's growth arc in specific detail. The specialist path wants to see *depth artifacts* — the technical write-up that another org's staff engineer cited, the open-source contribution that shifted a library's default, the internal talk that another team pattern-matched their own work against.
- **Bar-calibration behaviour.** When they sit in a review — an RFC review, a design review, a launch review — do they raise questions that would shift the artifact's *shape*, or questions that would shift the *plan* around it, or questions about how someone is *growing* through the work? Shape-shifting questions are the principal-IC signal. Plan-shifting questions are the EM signal. Growth-of-the-author questions are also the EM signal. Depth-of-the-approach questions are the specialist signal. All three are legitimate; the mix tells you where their instincts already sit.
- **Response to the ambiguous decision.** Give them a genuinely ambiguous org-scope question — *"the RecSys team wants to fork the feature platform. What should we do?"* — and watch how they navigate. Do they draft the paired-artifact deep-dive that positions the trade-offs and asks for a decision (IC signal)? Do they set up the meeting series that would surface consensus and get the team unblocked (EM signal)? Do they go deep into the two feature-platform-shape options and produce the depth-analysis that pre-computes the answer (specialist signal)? Again, all three responses are legitimate. What is *not* legitimate is a mentee who cannot form a first-instinct at all — that says they are still growing into staff scope, not that they are ready to pick a next path.

## Endorsement criteria — what you would need to see before endorsing each

Naming the criteria out loud is what makes the coaching legible. The mentee should know what signal you are reading for; that is what lets them go *build* the signal instead of guessing.

### To endorse the principal-IC path

- **A paired artifact that survived a real cycle.** The mod-410 exercise-02 exec-audience RFC (or a work equivalent) has been through one revision, one stakeholder round, and one substantive re-authoring — and the RFC's *shape* got better across the cycle, not merely the details. See mod-502 chapter 7 § *paired-artifact frame*; the L40 version is a rehearsal for the L48+ paired one-pager / deep-dive.
- **At least one cross-team ratification story where the mentee was the technical voice, not the sponsor.** The mentee walked into a room where an adjacent team was going to make an architecture call, framed the technical question in a way the room deferred to, and the resulting decision was better because of the framing. Not "I influenced the outcome"; "I framed the choice, the room chose".
- **A demonstrated instinct for the *artifact* over the *meeting*.** When something is unclear, does the mentee's first move produce a document (IC signal) or produce a meeting (EM signal)? Both are useful; the IC-principal path selects hard for the document-first instinct because the artifact is the leverage.
- **A rejection of the shortcut.** The mentee has, at least once, turned down a fast-track opportunity that would have compromised the technical quality of the artifact — declined to ship an RFC without the eval section, declined to sign off on a launch without the load-test data. Chapter 4 § *the review bar* is the practice; the L40 rejection is the seed of the L48+ bar.

### To endorse the engineering-manager path

- **A story about coaching a specific engineer through a specific growth moment.** Not "I mentored several juniors"; a specific engineer, a specific stuck-point, a specific unlock, a specific outcome. The mentee can describe *the engineer's* arc — what they were working on before, what they are working on after — in more detail than they can describe their own.
- **Repeated evidence that the mentee's energy is replenished by the people work.** Ask them directly: *"the meeting where you help engineer X figure out their promo case — is that the meeting that recharges you, or the meeting you push to the end of the day?"* Push through the socially-desirable answer.
- **A recent choice to prioritise a person over an artifact.** The mentee has, at least once, delayed shipping their own work to be present for a report or peer who needed them — and does not describe it as a sacrifice.
- **Comfort with the *ambiguity* of people-work.** The EM path involves calls where the technically-correct answer is not the right answer for the person; the mentee has seen this and can talk about it without treating it as a bug.

Note: the endorsement is *for the EM path*, not for *your team's EM opening*. The mentee's next EM seat is likely somewhere else — chapter 5 § *boundary with the peer EM track* is why the principal does not staff their team's EM seat; that is the EM's manager's call. The IC-principal is *not* the hiring manager for a first-line EM.

### To endorse the specialist path

- **A depth-artifact that a peer specialist outside the mentee's team cited.** The technical write-up, the open-source contribution, the internal talk. Cited *for its depth*, not merely referenced.
- **A demonstrated preference for depth over breadth in the face of a real trade-off.** The mentee has, at least once, turned down a broadening role or a scope-expansion opportunity because it would have pulled them off the axis they are going deep on. Not indecisive; deliberate.
- **A named axis.** They can name the axis in one sentence and defend the choice for 20 minutes. *"I am the person our org would ask about ranking-loss decomposition and multi-objective training-signal fusion."* Not *"I am really into ML systems".*
- **A peer specialist elsewhere in the industry who is what the mentee wants to be at 45.** A specific person, ideally with a specific title and a specific artifact portfolio the mentee has read end-to-end. If the mentee cannot name one, the axis is either too broad or not fully chosen.

Note: the specialist track is not this track. Endorsement here means *pointing them at a track that is not yours* — one of the peer specialist tracks — and being explicit about that. Coaching a specialist candidate as if they were a generalist-IC candidate is a failure mode chapter 5 § *technical-culture leverage* returns to.

## The shape of the conversation

Not the script — the shape. Adjust to the mentee. Assume roughly 60–90 minutes; run it two or three times over a quarter, not once.

1. **Frame the choice, not the answer.** Open by naming the three paths and the fact that the mentee is choosing which one to *invest* the next two years toward — not which one to *be* immediately. The choice compresses if framed as terminal.
2. **Walk the three archetypes as they operate at L48+.** Use the day-shape descriptions above. Do not sell any of them; describe them.
3. **Ask the mentee to place themselves against the four axes.** Energy pattern, portfolio evidence, bar-calibration behaviour, response to the ambiguous decision. Their placement matters; yours is a check, not the primary signal.
4. **Read the placement back with your own read.** *"When you describe the meeting that leaves you charged, you are describing the EM signal. When you describe your last RFC, you are describing IC signal. That's a genuine split; here is how to break it."* Do not resolve their ambiguity for them; make it visible so they can resolve it.
5. **Name the endorsement criteria for the path they are leaning toward.** Concretely. *"To endorse you for the principal-IC path, I need to see the next RFC go through one revision cycle where the shape of the document changes materially, not just the details. Ship the first draft in a month; let's review in two."*
6. **Set the artifact.** The mentee leaves the conversation with a document — a personal-development plan, a track-specific portfolio-build plan, a mod-410 exercise-01 next-cycle addendum. Something *written* that the next 1:1 revises.
7. **Set the cadence.** Six weeks to next check-in for path-leaning conversations; three months once the path is chosen and the artifact-build is underway. Chapter 6 § *personal-cadence artifact* is the reference for how you keep the mentee's arc legible in your own quarterly reflection.

## Failure modes

- **Coaching L30, not L40.** Mod-410 exercise-04 owns coaching the L30 senior IC through their first multi-team blueprint. If your calendar is full of L30 mentees, you are doing staff-scope work at principal-scope opportunity cost. The fix: identify the staff engineer's line-EM as the correct owner of the L30 conversation, and *coach the L40 mentee on how to run it themselves*. Mod-501 chapter 6 (hand-off contracts) is the seam.
- **Coaching your favorites.** The staff engineers whose energy patterns most match yours are the ones you find easiest to coach. They are not necessarily the ones who most need principal coaching — sometimes they are the ones who need the least. The fix: audit the last four quarters of your L40 1:1s. If the same three names dominate, ask why. Chapter 5 § *technical-culture leverage* returns to the org-wide fairness angle.
- **Coaching toward the answer you have already decided on.** The pattern where the principal has already concluded the mentee is (or is not) IC-principal material and runs the conversation to ratify the conclusion. This is unfair to the mentee and — practically — the principal's early read is wrong often enough that it should be a hypothesis, not a diagnosis. The fix: state your hypothesis out loud in the conversation and invite the mentee to falsify it.
- **Sponsorship-as-service, not coaching.** Sponsorship is the specific act of putting your credibility behind the mentee in a room they are not in — recommending them for the staff-plus loop chair role, nominating them for the internal cross-team RFC review body, naming them as your successor on the North Star artifact. It is a distinct action from coaching and should follow the coaching, not substitute for it. The fix: reserve sponsorship for after the endorsement criteria are met; do not spend it to compensate for coaching gaps.
- **Sliding into first-line EM work.** The path where the principal starts running 1:1s with the staff engineer's directs, sitting in on their team's performance-review calibration, or adjudicating comp questions. The staff engineer is not your report; their directs are two rungs away. Chapter 5 § *boundary with the peer EM track* is the reference; the EM branch (`ai-infra-team-lead-learning` at the entry rung) owns that scope.
- **Endorsing the principal-IC path because it flatters you.** The most-cited principal-IC-path failure: the mentor endorses the path because the mentee mirrors them. Chapter 6 § *the "chasing every bet" drift* is the parent failure mode; this is one of its expressions.

## A worked micro-example

For grounding, here is what a *placement-summary paragraph* on a staff engineer growth conversation reads like at defensible fidelity. The full artifact is the mentee's, not yours; the paragraph below is what you carry into your own quarterly reflection (chapter 6).

> **L40 mentee M — placement notes (FY26-Q3, v2).** M is 4.5 years at L40, currently the technical lead on the RecSys re-platform (2 teams, 11 engineers). Energy pattern: charged by the paired-artifact review cycle, drained by the cross-team planning meeting. Portfolio: two shipped exec-audience RFCs (last cycle's ranking-loss and next cycle's real-time features), both went through 3+ revisions where the *shape* changed. Bar-calibration behaviour: shape-shifting questions in review, mixed with a real fluency in the RecSys depth axis. Response to the ambiguous decision (feature-platform-fork question I gave): drafted a 6-page paired analysis in 8 days, presented as a decision-ask; did not attempt to convene a meeting. Placement read: principal-IC path leaning, with a real specialist alternative on the RecSys axis. Endorsement criteria set for principal-IC: next RFC (feature-platform decision) revised at least once at the shape level; one cross-team ratification story where M was the technical voice, not the sponsor, by Q1 FY27. Alternative-path check: pointed M at [`senior-ml-engineer-learning`](https://github.com/ml-engineering-curriculum/senior-ml-engineer-learning) mod-306 and the RecSys track lead at a peer org to sanity-check the specialist alternative. Next 1:1: 2026-11-05.

Notice what is *not* in the paragraph: adjectives, character judgments, comparisons to other mentees, statements about M's manager. What *is* in it: the artifacts, the observed behaviours, the endorsement criteria, the alternative-path check, the next date. That is the standard the coaching practice has to hit for M to be able to steer their own path.

## Summary

- Mod-501 chapter 5 § *Theme 4* named the object of the principal's *coach* verb as staff engineers (L40) and staff-plus hiring committees. This chapter walks the L40 half.
- Three paths open from L40: principal-IC (this track), engineering-manager (peer branch, `ai-infra-team-lead-learning` at the entry rung), specialist (peer specialist tracks — RecSys, performance, fine-tuning, security, agentic-systems, RAG). The paths look nearly identical at L40 and radically different at L48+.
- The principal reads the mentee against four axes: energy pattern, portfolio evidence, bar-calibration behaviour, response to the ambiguous decision. Fifth axis (personal-life fit) is the mentee's, not yours.
- The endorsement criteria for each path are named out loud so the mentee can *build* the signal, not guess at it. Principal-IC selects hard for paired-artifact instincts and document-first energy; EM selects hard for people-work replenishment and comfort with people-ambiguity; specialist selects hard for depth-artifact evidence and a named axis.
- The conversation frames the choice, walks the three archetypes as they operate at L48+, reads the mentee's placement, names the criteria, sets an artifact, and sets a cadence. It runs multiple times across a quarter, not once.
- Six failure modes: coaching L30 not L40, coaching your favorites, coaching toward a decided answer, sponsorship-as-service substitution, sliding into first-line EM work, endorsing principal-IC because it flatters you.
- Chapter 2 walks the *other* half of Theme 4: multiplying the effectiveness of every ML team via a small set of leverage moves — Upstart's *"multiply the effectiveness of every ML team"*.
