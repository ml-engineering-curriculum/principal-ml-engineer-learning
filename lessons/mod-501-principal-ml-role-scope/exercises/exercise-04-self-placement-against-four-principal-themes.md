# exercise-04: Self-Placement Against the Four Principal-Scope Themes

**Estimated effort:** 2 hours

## Objective

Produce the five-by-five self-assessment from chapter 5: for each of the four principal-scope themes at ≥0.60 in-window frequency (plus the below-threshold industry-influence theme), name where you have lived experience already, where the level-up work is, which module lifts you, and how you'll know you're done. The assessment is a working document you carry across the rest of this track and revisit at the end of each module.

Simultaneously, produce the hand-off contract from chapter 6: the three-list artifact naming staff-owned, principal-owned, and jointly-owned artifacts *for your current or target role*, with an escalation path. Even if you don't hold a Family-1 role yet, drafting the contract against your target role sharpens what mod-502 through mod-506 mean for you specifically.

## Prerequisites

- Chapters 5 and 6 read.
- Exercises 01 (verb audit), 02 (title map), 03 (family classification) completed. The vocabulary from those exercises is what you self-assess against.
- Willingness to write honestly about gaps. This is a private artifact.

## Problem statement

The rest of this track — mod-502 through mod-506, plus the three capstones — presumes you know where you sit relative to the four principal-scope themes. If you don't, mod-502 through mod-505 will feel abstract in some sections and repetitive in others. The self-assessment is the calibration document that lets you spend attention where the level-up work actually is.

The hand-off contract is the working artifact chapter 6 recommended. It matters most once you hold the role, but drafting it *before* helps you spot the lift-vs-redo trap in advance: which staff-scope artifacts would you feel pulled to keep authoring, which would you cede to the staff engineers underneath you, and what is the escalation path when a staff engineer disagrees with your call.

## Requirements

Produce two markdown files in a personal workspace:

1. **`exercise-04-self-assessment.md`** — the five-by-five grid.
2. **`exercise-04-handoff-contract.md`** — the three-list hand-off contract with an escalation path.

### File 1: five-by-five self-assessment

Structure it as one section per theme, five theme sections total. For each theme, five sub-cells:

1. **The theme statement, in your own words.** One sentence. If you can't state it in your own words, re-read the corresponding theme section in chapter 5.
2. **Lived experience — the strongest artifact you already have.** Specific. A document you authored, a program you led, a hiring loop you calibrated. Not a story about the theme; the artifact itself. Include a one-sentence description of the artifact and, if available, a link (private repo, internal doc, or public artifact — whichever is appropriate for your situation).
3. **The gap between where you are and the theme's principal-altitude object.** Concrete and pointed. Not "I need to think more strategically" — instead, "I have led portfolio-scope alignment across three teams but never SVP-level, so I have never had to shape the material my VP would use to bring an SVP along."
4. **The learning move.** Which module lifts you on this theme (theme 1 → mod-502, theme 2 → mod-503, theme 3 → mod-504, theme 4 → mod-505, theme 5 → mod-506). Which project integrates it. And, critically, what field opportunity — current or upcoming — will let you practice at principal altitude, not just study. If no field opportunity is available, name the constructed alternative you'll build (e.g., an internal doc + review with a mentor + retrospective).
5. **The retirement criterion.** How will you know you've lifted from level-up to lived-experience on this theme? Name a specific artifact or specific outcome. If you cannot name a criterion, you cannot tell when you're done.

At the end of the file, a two-paragraph reflection:

- **First paragraph:** which themes did you find yourself with strongest lived experience on? Which weakest? Does the shape match chapter 5's normal-case guidance (theme 4 mentorship strongest, theme 1 partial, themes 2/3/5 level-up)? If your shape is unusual, why?
- **Second paragraph:** if you had to defend the assessment to a peer principal engineer or a mentor — someone likely to push back — which cell is weakest? Which cell would you strengthen with a follow-up artifact or field-opportunity move in the next 30 days? Name one concrete move.

### File 2: three-list hand-off contract

Aimed at your current or target Family-1 role. If you already hold a Family-1 role, use it. If not, pick a plausible target (an employer + specific rung from your exercise-02 title map, or the shape of the role you're preparing for).

Structure it as follows:

- **Header** — the role you're drafting against (employer + title + product area, or "target role, generic").
- **Staff-owned artifacts** — a bulleted list of 6–10 artifacts. For each: what it is, which staff engineer role (`staff-ml-engineer-learning` module or peer specialist track) owns it, and one sentence on the principal's relationship to it (usually "review and adjudicate", sometimes "provide input for").
- **Principal-owned artifacts** — a bulleted list of 4–8 artifacts. For each: what it is, which module in this track lifts you toward it, and one sentence on how staff engineers under you contribute inputs (usually cited underlayers, sometimes review).
- **Jointly-owned artifacts** — a bulleted list of 2–4 items. Chapter 6 names two — the staff-plus hiring loop rubric and the sunset-criteria list for retired bets. Add employer-specific joint items if you have visibility into your target role's org chart.
- **Escalation path for disagreement** — one paragraph. Chapter 6's suggested shape: staff engineer surfaces disagreement in the design review; if unresolved, principal calls a decision; if the staff engineer still disagrees, path escalates to the VP (not to skip the principal). Adapt to your target role's actual reporting chain if you know it.

At the end of the file, a one-paragraph reflection: which artifact in the contract would you feel most pulled to re-author yourself if you took on the role tomorrow? Name it. What move — during this track — would prepare you to *not* re-author it, and instead lift?

## Starter guidance

- Write the self-assessment first. The hand-off contract is easier to draft once you know where your own strengths sit.
- For lived-experience cells, if you can't cite an artifact by name and date, treat that as absence of lived experience. Vague memory of "having done something like this" is not lived experience; the artifact is.
- For the gap cells, resist the temptation to make the gap feel small. This is a private artifact; over-generosity is your enemy. Chapter 5's normal-case guidance is *"lived experience on all five themes"* being suspicious. Do not fall into that.
- The retirement criteria should be a specific artifact or a specific outcome — "I will have authored and defended the North Star document to my SVP", "I will have calibrated a staff-plus hiring loop and adjudicated at least one interviewer split", "I will have shipped an industry-influence piece that another org's engineer cited in a public artifact". Not "I will feel confident on this theme".
- The hand-off contract can feel abstract if you don't hold the role yet. That's fine — draft it against a plausible target. The pull-to-re-author reflection at the end is what makes the exercise worth doing pre-role.

## Acceptance criteria

Your two documents are done when they can pass all of the following:

- [ ] Five themes covered in the self-assessment (four at ≥0.60 plus theme 5).
- [ ] Every theme has a lived-experience artifact cited or explicitly noted as absent.
- [ ] Every theme has a concrete gap statement (not a vague one).
- [ ] Every theme has a learning move that names both the module and either a field opportunity or a constructed alternative.
- [ ] Every theme has a retirement criterion — a specific artifact or outcome, not a feeling.
- [ ] The two-paragraph reflection at the end honestly identifies the weakest cell and names a concrete 30-day follow-up move.
- [ ] The hand-off contract has all three lists filled in for a specific current or target role.
- [ ] Chapter 6's two jointly-owned items (technical-bar rubric, sunset-criteria list) appear in the joint list, plus any employer-specific additions.
- [ ] The escalation path names a specific reporting chain (not "escalate to leadership").
- [ ] The pull-to-re-author reflection names a specific artifact and a specific in-track move that prepares you to lift instead of re-do.

## Stretch goals

- **Peer or mentor review.** Have a peer principal engineer, a staff engineer at your target level, or a mentor read the self-assessment and pressure-test the lived-experience cells. Which ones do they push back on? Which ones do they confirm? Update the assessment.
- **30-day follow-up log.** Alongside the assessment, keep a short markdown log — the concrete follow-up move you named in the reflection, plus a check-in at 30 days on whether it happened, what came out of it, and whether the weakest cell has moved. This is the discipline mod-505 asks you to sustain over years; starting the log now makes it a habit rather than a lift later.
- **Second-pass hand-off contract.** Draft the same three lists for a plausibly-different target role (a different segment — if your target is a Family-1 fintech Distinguished role, draft a second contract for a Family-1 consumer / recsys Principal role). What shifts? Which artifacts are load-bearing in one role and not the other?

## Deliverable location

Local workspace. Do not commit into this curriculum repo. These are per-learner artifacts, and their value is precisely in their honesty. Revisit at the end of each subsequent module (mod-502, 503, 504, 505, 506) and update the lived-experience cells and retirement-criteria checks as you go.
